Topic 6

🚪 Tailgating

Following someone into a secure area.

Definition

Tailgating is a physical social-engineering attack. An unauthorized person follows an authorized person through a locked door or checkpoint to enter a restricted area without an ID or access card.

How it works

The attacker takes advantage of politeness and busy entrances. They may carry boxes, wear a uniform, pretend to have forgotten a card, or act as though they belong there.

Easy example

An employee opens a secure office door. A stranger carrying boxes says, “Please hold the door; my card is inside.” The employee lets the stranger enter without identification.

Warning signs

  • Stranger asks you to hold a secure door
  • Person claims to have forgotten an ID
  • Someone follows closely through a checkpoint
  • Person avoids security staff

How to stay safe

Do not allow unknown people through secure doors. Ask security or authorized staff to verify the visitor, and ensure the door closes behind you.

Notebook questions

  1. What is tailgating?
  2. Why might an attacker carry boxes?
  3. How can an organization prevent it?
